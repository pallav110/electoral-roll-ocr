The status badge was previously inlined as the same five-line if/elif chain in documents.html, document.html and dashboard.html, and a fourth copy would have arrived with the uploads table. status_tone() already returns the right colour key for every status; this is the one place that turns a tone into classes. Classes here are the semantic ones from base.html (`.pill`, `.is-bad`) rather than Tailwind colour utilities. Two reasons: the hand-written fallback stylesheet would otherwise need a copy of every Tailwind shade in existence, and a tailwind bg/text pair has a failure mode this project already hit -- the badge is a inside a flex row, and a `display` utility on it silently overrides the inline-flex that puts the dot and the label on one line. Semantic classes have one author and one meaning. Two badges exist and the difference is deliberate: status_badge() the original `.pill`, colour + label. Still used by the other pages, which are not part of this change. state_badge() the new `.badge`, which adds a per-tone glyph so the state survives being printed in greyscale or read by someone who cannot separate the hues. That second point is a real accessibility requirement, not decoration. The Carbon status-indicator guidance is explicit that colour alone is insufficient and that an indicator needs at least three of symbol, shape, colour and type; `.pill` carried only two of them. `.badge` carries all four -- a distinct mark per tone, a distinct fill shape, the tone colour, and the written label. #} {# The glyph per tone.
